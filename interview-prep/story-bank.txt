>>> HOW TO USE THIS FILE <<<
The three stories below are fictional examples showing the expected format.
Delete them and add your own after each interview or prep session. What
matters structurally:
  - One story per block, with a descriptive STORY: title
  - A "Competencies:" line so tailoring can match stories to questions
  - SOAR body: Situation, Obstacle, Action, Result
  - Optional tagged variants where you have told the same story two ways
  - A flagged discrepancy note when two versions genuinely conflict, so the
    conflict gets resolved rather than silently picked from

STORY: Replatforming without stopping delivery
===================================================================
Competencies: Delivery leadership, technical judgement, stakeholder management

Situation: The team owned a service running on infrastructure the company
was retiring, with a hard cutoff date twelve months out.
Obstacle: Product had a committed roadmap for the same period, so there was
no window to pause feature work for the migration.
Action: Split the migration into increments that could ship behind a flag
alongside feature work, and agreed with product that roughly one third of
each sprint was ring-fenced for migration.
Result: Cutover completed a month ahead of the deadline with no unplanned
downtime, and the roadmap slipped by weeks rather than being abandoned.

===================================================================
STORY: Holding a release
===================================================================
Competencies: Quality ownership, decision making under pressure

Situation: A monthly release was ready on the scheduled date, but a defect
surfaced in a payment edge case the day before.
Obstacle: The date had been communicated to customers, and the defect was
low frequency, so there was real pressure to ship and patch later.
Action: Quantified the exposure (how many accounts could hit it, what the
failure did), presented that rather than an opinion, and recommended a
two-day hold.
Result: The hold was agreed, the fix shipped with the release, and the
exposure figures became a standard part of subsequent go/no-go calls.

[Variant — told with a people emphasis]
Same event, framed around the conversation with the product lead who wanted
to ship, and how disagreement got resolved with data instead of seniority.
Use this variant when the question is about influencing or conflict rather
than about quality gates.

STORY: Coaching an engineer through a difficult first year
===================================================================
Competencies: Mentoring, people development, feedback

Situation: A junior engineer joined the team and was strong technically but
consistently over-committed and missed sprint goals.
Obstacle: Direct feedback had already been given by a previous lead and had
not changed the pattern, and confidence was visibly dropping.
Action: Moved from feedback to structured practice, sizing work together in
1:1s before sprint planning for six weeks, then handing the estimate back.
Result: Commitments became reliable within a quarter, and the engineer went
on to own a service independently.

⚠ FLAGGED — two conflicting versions of the same event
If one telling of a story says a team was six people and another says nine,
record both versions here with this flag rather than picking one. Interview
prep should surface the conflict so it gets checked, since a detail that
shifts between tellings is exactly what a second interviewer notices.
